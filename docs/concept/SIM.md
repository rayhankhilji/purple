# CONCEPT lap simulator (Silverstone), v2

Every output carries this label: **"physics lap sim · quasi-steady-state point mass · Silverstone OSM centreline · calibrated to 2026 British GP qualifying"** (the label string is kept for its consumers; v2 resolves loads per axle, see §3).

This is the deterministic benchmark the car-design optimiser calls. It computes one flying qualifying lap (time, sectors, speed / gear / rpm / time trace, per-corner speeds, energy use) from a car's physical numbers: mass, aero coefficients per active-aero mode and the aero platform, PU power and the 2026 energy rules, tyres, brakes and gearing. **Speeds come out of the physics and are never fitted as targets, and there are no per-corner factors anywhere.** Telemetry is used for two things only:

1. Estimating parameters nobody publishes, on **one** lap (the pole lap's speed trace and lap time), **inside the sourced prior ranges** of `concept/data/priors-2026.json`.
2. A **held-out check** on 10 other teams' laps, scored with the track model's `compare()` (`concept/track/track.js`).

Per-team car parameters are inferred from public lap times and speed traps. They are not team data.

Code is in `concept/sim/` (plain JS for Node and the browser). Tests: `test/concept-sim.test.js`, `test/concept-sim-v2.test.js`. Overlay figures: `docs/concept/sim-trace-{ant,had,sai}.svg`.

## 1. Contract

```js
simulateLap(car /* PhysicalCar */, "silverstone", conditions?, opts?) →
{ lap_s, sectors_s: [s1, s2, s3], vmax_kmh, speed_trap_kmh, vmin_kmh,
  energy: { deployed_mj, harvested_mj, harvested_braking_mj, harvested_superclip_mj, harvested_part_throttle_mj, recharge_cap_mj,
            superclip_share, superclip_fill, energy_limited, soc_start_mj, soc_min_mj, soc_end_mj, strategy, deploy_ceiling_kmh },
  speeds_kmh: { intermediate_1, intermediate_2, finish_line }, air_density_kgm3, label,
  corners: [{ n, name, marker_m, min_kmh, min_at_m, apex_kmh, apex_radius_m, apex_lat_g }],   // 18 MultiViewer corners
  trace? /* opts.trace */: { s_m[], v_kmh[], t_s[], aero_mode[], gear[]?, rpm[]? } }       // s_m, t_s from the control line
contributions(car, refCar = baselineCar(), conditions?, opts?) →
  { lap_s, ref_lap_s, delta_s, segments: [{ kind: "corner"|"straight", name, s0_m, s1_m, time_s, ref_time_s, delta_s, min_kmh, ref_min_kmh }] }
baselineCar(teamId?), calibrate(opts?), fitTeams(opts?), heldOut(fit, dir), teamCar(id), teamCars(), cornerTable(car?), fitted(), LABEL
```

- **`conditions`:** `{ air_temp_c, track_temp_c, air_density_kgm3?, air_pressure_hpa?, humidity_pct?, wind_ms, wind_from_deg, wet, fuel_kg?, recharge_limit_mj?, es_start_mj? }`. Defaults: 2026 British GP qualifying (§4.4). `es_start_mj` is the store at the start of the flying lap (default the full 4 MJ window); only the `"optimal"` strategy tracks the store within the lap.
- **`opts`:** `trace`, `strategy` (`"threshold"` default; `"optimal"`, `"marginal"`), `overtake`, `no_superclip`, `part_throttle_harvest` (optimal only, default true), `line` (`"telemetry"` default, `"osm"`), `grade_scale` (default 1; 0 = flat track, for studies), `cal` (identity).
- **`contributions`:** the lap is cut into corner zones (each marker −100 / +100 m, never past halfway to a neighbour) and the straights between; each segment's time comes from the two cars' own time traces, so the segment deltas add up to the lap delta (to rounding). This is the per-segment explanation of a design change.
- **PhysicalCar fields the sim reads** (all read defensively; new in v2 marked †): `mass_kg, fuel_kg, wheelbase_m`†(longitudinal load transfer)`, track_front_m, track_rear_m, cog_height_m, weight_dist_front, drivetrain_eff`; `aero.corner|straight.{CLA_front_m2, CLA_rear_m2, CDA_m2}`, `aero.floor_stalled` (or top-level), `aero.ride_sens_per_mm`†, `aero.stall_margin_mm`†, `aero.heave_n_per_mm`† (optional), `aero.floor_share`† (optional), `aero.yaw_sens`† (optional, default 0); `pu.{ice_kw, mguk_kw, es_deploy_mj_per_lap, es_harvest_mj_per_lap, superclip_kw?}`; `tyres.{mu_x, mu_y, load_sens}`; `brakes.max_decel_g_mech`; `gearing.{rpm_per_kmh[8], shift_rpm}` (now also drives the upshift cut). `ride_height_mm` and `session` are not read (CLA is taken at the static ride height already).
- **Determinism:** fixed iteration counts, Float64, no randomness, no network, no Mubit, never touches `data/` or `data-mock/`.

## 2. Track (corner by corner)

- **Racing line:** the mean path of the 22 qualifying laps (OpenF1 X/Y, `concept/data/telemetry`), averaged at equal lap fractions and mapped into the OSM frame of `js/data/silverstone.js` (Procrustes: reflection, 1.38° rotation, 5.3 m RMS). Smoothed with σ = one position-sample interval of travel (max(6 m, v·0.26 s)); curvature = heading change per metre, σ = 4 m. Per-segment curvature on a 2.5 m grid (2330 points); the per-corner radii are in `cornerTable()` and the `corners` output. The line radii are insensitive to the smoothing choice at the corners that matter (Stowe 83–89 m, Brooklands 57–59 m, Becketts T13 84–89 m for line σ ×1–2 and curvature σ 4–10 m).
- **Track width and kerbs:** the telemetry line is the path the cars actually drove, kerbs included, so no width constraint is applied to it. The alternative `opts.line = "osm"` (minimum-peak-curvature line) keeps the car centre 1 m inside the 15 m OSM width (`js/data/silverstone.js`), which gives back roughly the kerb width; it is ~1.8 s slower (tighter fast corners).
- **Elevation / gradient:** the relative height profile in `js/data/silverstone.js` (12 m relief, 120 m spacing), mapped through the alignment. The repo does not document where that profile comes from (TRACK.md), so **gradient is an estimate**. Grade enters both passes as m·g·grade. Test: a steeper climb (`grade_scale` 3 vs −3) costs speed over the steepest climb.
- **Banking / camber:** **none modelled (0).** A search found no published banking or crossfall figures for Silverstone; it is a flat airfield circuit. Not invented.
- **Timing frame:** s = distance along the pole lap from the control line (5824.6 m). Sector lines 1807.5 / 4239.1 m (where the pole lap's time channel reaches S1, S1+S2), speed trap 4780 m (README fit of 11 traps, RMS 0.8 km/h), intermediates 1840 / 4170 m.
- **Straight Mode zones:** FIA activation points A1–A4 (concept/data/silverstone-2026-weekend.json); each runs to the next corner (deactivation points unpublished).

## 3. Model

Air speed u = v + headwind (the component of the wind along each segment's heading; same convention as `lab/aero.js`), q = ½ρu². **Every aero load and drag force is evaluated at u**, so a headwind raises drag and downforce together.

### 3.1 Aero platform (ride-height-dependent aero) — new

The aero load F squats the car by dh = F / k_heave. The CLA at the static ride height grows by `ride_sens_per_mm` per mm of squat (the design layer's d(df)/d(rh), concept/aeromap.js rideSensitivity; 0.6 %/mm at the baseline, inside the priors' 0.3–0.8 %/mm). Once the squat passes the stall onset (5 mm + the design's dynamic stall margin `stall_margin_mm`, 3.67 mm at the baseline), the floor's share of the load (0.55, priors 0.45–0.65) rolls off quadratically over 25 mm to 40 % (concept/aeromap.js FLOOR). The equilibrium squat is solved by bisection per table speed. k_heave = 3500 N/mm is an **assumption** chosen to match the design layer's own 5 mm dynamic compression at the ~17 kN high-speed load. Effect on the baseline: +1.3 % CLA at 200 km/h, +3 % at 300 km/h, no stall; a design that lowers its stall margin below ~−4 mm stalls the floor at speed (test). Straight Mode coefficients are still capped at the Corner Mode values (no exploit).

### 3.2 Tyres and axle grip with load transfer

- μ(F_z) = μ₀(1 + ls − ls·F_z/F_z0) per tyre (F_z0 = static tyre load), i.e. load sensitivity in % per kN = ls / F_z0[kN].
- **Lateral:** per axle, with lateral load transfer ΔF = m_axle·a_y·h/track solved in closed form; a_y,max is the smaller axle limit (a balance mismatch costs grip).
- **Longitudinal load transfer (new):** ΔF = m·a_x·h/wheelbase, fixed point on the car's own deceleration/acceleration: braking moves load to the front, traction to the driven rear axle. Ideal brake balance; braking capped by `max_decel_g_mech`.
- Friction ellipse couples a_x and a_y on corner entry (trail braking) and exit. Traction is rear-only.
- **Tyre slip drag (new):** m·a_y·tan(α), α = 0.08 rad × (share of the lateral limit used) (assumption, as concept/tools/derive-car.js). It makes the cars lose speed at full throttle through Abbey, Farm, Copse and Maggotts.
- **Track temperature (new, ESTIMATE):** μ × (1 − 1.3·10⁻⁴·(T − 40.4 °C)²), floor 0.85. No public 2026 Pirelli number was found; T_ref is the calibration session's track temperature, so the calibrated car is unchanged there (±15 °C costs ~3 %).
- **Crosswind yaw (new, ESTIMATE, off by default):** downforce × (1 − yaw_sens·|yaw°|), yaw = atan(crosswind / u), floor 0.5, with `aero.yaw_sens` from the PhysicalCar (default 0: contract request to the design layer).
- **Wet:** μ × 0.75, C_rr × 1.5.

### 3.3 Resistance and power

Drag ½ρu²·CDA, rolling 0.012·(m g + aero load), grade, slip drag. P_wheel = η_dt·(P_ICE(N) − P_clip/0.97) + η_dt·0.97·P_K; P_K ≤ min(350 kW, C5.2.8 taper); P_ICE(N) reduced below 10,500 rpm by (0.27N + 165)/3000 (C5.2.4). The K deploys only what traction can use.

**Gear shifts (new):** the lowest gear under `shift_rpm` (8 gears). Each upshift cuts all drive force for `MODEL.upshift_cut_s` = 30 ms (**assumption**: 2026 seamless-shift gearboxes cut very little; capped at 200 ms), consumed in fractions of a step and with a held-gear hysteresis, so the lap stays a smooth function of the car.

### 3.4 Energy deployment strategies

The 2026 rules modelled: C5.2.7 (350 kW), C5.2.8 taper, C5.2.9 4 MJ window, C5.2.10 recharge cap (6.5 MJ in Silverstone qualifying), C5.2.21 (0.97).

- **`"threshold"` (default, behavioural):** the K deploys below a speed ceiling v\*, and superclips above it at a uniform power share filling a fraction φ of what braking leaves of the recharge cap (φ by a 5-point golden-section search for the fastest lap); v\* by bisection so that deployed ≤ 4 MJ + harvested. It reproduces the speed plateau of the real 2026 traps.
- **`"optimal"` (new, principled):** deploy and superclip are chosen by the lap-time value of a joule — the costate of kinetic energy computed by an adjoint backward pass on a reference lap (Σ ds/v³ carried back along each straight with the drag and power derivatives, reset where grip sets the speed). Deploy where the value ≥ x, superclip where it ≤ min(x·0.97², cap-filling level), with a soft (logistic) switch so the lap is smooth in the car. Free harvest: the rear-axle share of the brake force and, at part throttle, the ICE surplus over what the corner lets the car use. **The store is tracked along the lap from the control line** (start = `es_start_mj`, default 4 MJ): it may never run empty, harvest is stored only below the top of the window and below the lap's recharge cap; x is set by bisection on that ledger. Output: `soc_min_mj`, `soc_end_mj`.
- **Why "optimal" is not the default:** with the in-prior car it deploys hard on every straight and only clips at the very end, so the Hangar Straight peaks at ~330 km/h where every real car holds ~303 km/h; held-out RMS was ~15 km/h and the trap +20 km/h in development runs (against threshold's ~12.4 and +4 for the team cars). Real 2026 deployment is further shaped by an event rule we do not model: Silverstone's FIA PU document lists a **50 kW/s maximum PU power-reduction rate (C5.12.8)** with exempt sectors (concept/data/silverstone-2026-weekend.json `energy_limits`). A rate-limited optimal controller would plateau and fade like the real traces; adding it is the most promising next step (§7).
- `"marginal"` is kept for study.

### 3.5 Passes

Apex limits from a_y,max by bisection (with the yaw-corrected grip when relevant); backward braking pass in Corner Mode; forward pass from the slowest apex with the strategy. Straight Mode only inside an FIA zone, after a ≤ 400 ms switch, closed when lateral demand exceeds 50 % of Straight Mode grip and always under braking.

**Smoothness:** a 0.25 % CLA step moves the lap by ≤ 0.035 s (test); the threshold strategy's trap can step by up to ~5 km/h between nearby designs (discrete φ search). Pure physics (energy not limiting) is smooth to ~0.014 s per 0.1 % step.

## 5. Estimation on one lap (no correction factors)

MAP, Nelder–Mead (420 iterations + 2 restarts, deterministic) over 8 parameters (μ_y, μ_x, CLA, balance, CDA, CDA_straight, CLA straight ratio, load sensitivity). Each is mapped to its prior range by a logistic transform (hard bounds) with a Gaussian prior centred on the central value, sd = range/4; CDA_straight lives in the intersection of its own range and CDA_corner × the ratio range. Objective: speed RMS on the pole lap's 5 m grid² + 400·(lap error)² + 10·(prior + data terms), data terms = the derived lateral limits and CdA (§4.2) and the Brembo 2026 Silverstone peak deceleration (3.4 g at 260 km/h, priors 3.1–3.8). `global = {1, 1}`.

**Fit lap (ANT, 88.111 s):** sim 88.115 s (+0.004), sectors 28.017 / 36.126 / 23.971 against 28.348 / 35.521 / 24.242, trap 314.7 against 302 km/h, speed RMS 13.1 km/h. The trap is not a fit target; the +13 km/h is the straight-line side of the grip limit in §7.

## 7. Limitations (biggest first)

1. **Mid-speed grip against the priors.** Inside the prior ranges (μ ≤ 2.0 at 2 kN, CLA ≤ 4.6 m², load sensitivity ≥ 3 %/kN) with lateral load transfer, the lateral limit cannot reach what the cars do at 150–240 km/h (Brooklands, Becketts T13, Stowe: 20–25 km/h slow) while the slow corners (Village, Vale) are already fast. The fit therefore puts μ_y at the top and load sensitivity near the bottom of their ranges, and the lap time is made up on the straights (trap +13 km/h on the fit lap). A wide-bounds diagnostic fit (μ to 2.8, CLA to 7, load sensitivity to 0) did not get below ~11.8 km/h either, because limitation 2 remains. What would close it is a model or prior change, not a factor: larger ride-height gain (more squat than the design layer's 5 mm), lower operating load sensitivity, or a higher CLA prior for 2026 cars.
2. **Straight-line energy shape.** Real traces plateau and fade on every straight (the TRACK.md clipping table); neither strategy reproduces it everywhere (Stowe→Vale +36 km/h peak, ends of the pit straight and Wellington +20). The likely missing rule is the C5.12.8 50 kW/s power-reduction-rate limit with its exempt sectors (in the weekend file, not modelled: its semantics are only partly documented). With it, the `"optimal"` strategy should become the default.
3. **Geometry estimates:** gradient from an undocumented elevation profile; no banking; a single averaged line for every car.
4. **Quasi-steady state:** no yaw or transient dynamics, no tyre temperature build-up or wear, ideal brake balance; yaw inertia, unsprung masses and material flex (now in the PhysicalCar) are not used.
5. **Team cars:** driver, session, tow and strategy are folded into three scales per team.

## 8. Timing and regeneration

- One lap: ~21 ms CPU (threshold), ~11 ms (optimal) in Node 22 after warm-up; test < 30 ms. The first call builds the track in ~1 s.
- `node concept/sim/build-fit.js` re-estimates, refits and re-checks in ~45 s, byte-deterministic; `--check` exits non-zero if `fitted.js` is stale. `node concept/sim/plot-trace.js ANT HAD SAI` redraws the overlays. `node concept/sim/build-line.js` rebuilds the line.
- Telemetry comes from OpenF1 (openf1.org), an independent community project not associated with Formula 1, the FIA or FOM, used for non-commercial research.
