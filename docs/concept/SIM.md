# CONCEPT lap simulator (Silverstone)

Label on every output: **"physics lap sim · quasi-steady-state point mass · Silverstone OSM centreline · calibrated to 2026 British GP qualifying"**

This is the deterministic benchmark the car-design optimiser calls. It computes the time, speed trace and energy use of one flying qualifying lap from a car's physical numbers (mass, aero coefficients, power, tyres). It is a physics model with two global calibration factors. It is not team data, and nobody should read it as a prediction of a real car's lap time beyond the stated residuals.

Code: `concept/sim/` (plain JS, runs in Node and the browser). Tests: `test/concept-sim.test.js`.

| File | Role |
|---|---|
| `track.js` | Loads `js/data/silverstone.js` (EI.TRACKS, read-only, isolated `vm` context with a `window` shim, no `document`). Handles resampling, the racing line, curvature, grade and the Straight Mode zones. Cached after the first build (~0.9 s). |
| `lapsim.js` | `simulateLap`, `baselineCar`, the regulation constants (REGS), the model assumptions (MODEL), default conditions and the timing frame |
| `fit.js` | `calibrate()` (global factors) and `fitTeams()` (competitor model), plus `teamCar(id)` and `teamCars()` |
| `fitted.js` | GENERATED by `node concept/sim/build-fit.js`. Holds the global factors, the 11 inferred team cars and their residuals. `--check` verifies it is current. |
| `index.js` | Public API (Node: `require("./concept/sim/index.js")`; browser: load `js/data/silverstone.js`, then `concept/sim/{track,lapsim,fitted,fit,index}.js` → `window.EI.conceptSim`). Adds `cornerTable()`. |

## 1. Contract

```js
simulateLap(car /* PhysicalCar */, "silverstone", conditions?, opts?) →
{ lap_s, sectors_s: [s1, s2, s3], vmax_kmh, speed_trap_kmh, vmin_kmh,
  energy: { deployed_mj, harvested_mj, harvested_braking_mj, harvested_superclip_mj, recharge_cap_mj,
            superclip_share, energy_limited, strategy, deploy_ceiling_kmh },
  speeds_kmh: { intermediate_1, intermediate_2, finish_line },   // FIA timing points
  air_density_kgm3, label,
  trace? /* opts.trace */: { s_m[], v_kmh[], aero_mode[] } }    // s_m from the control line
```

- `conditions`: `{ air_temp_c, track_temp_c, air_density_kgm3?, air_pressure_hpa?, humidity_pct?, wind_ms, wind_from_deg, wet, fuel_kg?, recharge_limit_mj? }`. Any field left out takes its value from `DEFAULT_CONDITIONS`, which are the 2026 British GP qualifying conditions (§4.4).
- `opts`: `trace`, `strategy: "threshold" | "optimal"` (§3.5), `overtake` (C5.2.8 ii curve), `no_superclip`, `cal` (override the global factors).
- PhysicalCar fields are used as specified. Additive fields are read defensively: `floor_stalled` (true → CLA × 0.5) and `stall_margin_mm` (informational). CLA values are taken as already evaluated at `aero.ride_height_mm` (the design layer, `concept/aeromap.js`, does that), so `ride_sens_per_mm` is informational and is not applied a second time. `cog_height_m` is the height above ground (plank and ride height included).
- Fully deterministic: fixed iteration counts, Float64 throughout, no randomness, no network, no Mubit.

## 2. Track

- **Centreline:** 506 OSM points (`EI.TRACKS.silverstone.center`, lap 5880.7 m against the FIA's 5891 m). They are resampled to 2,342 points (2.51 m), Gaussian-smoothed (σ = 10 m) to remove OSM vertex kinks, then resampled again.
- **Racing line:** a lateral offset q(s) along the centreline normal, with |q| ≤ 15/2 − 1.0 m. It minimises Σ w·|P(i−1) − 2P(i) + P(i+1)|² by projected gradient descent with iteratively reweighted curvature (w ∝ |κ|², which leans the classic minimum-curvature line toward minimum peak curvature). The solve runs coarse-to-fine at 40/20/10/5/2.5 m. Tripling the iterations changes no apex radius by more than 1 m. The racing line is 5762.7 m long.
- **Curvature:** heading change per metre on the racing line, then Gaussian smoothing (σ = 4 m). Grade comes from the data's elevation profile (12 m of relief).
- **Timing frame (FIA):** the control line is at data s = 0, not at the game's `startFinish` 260 m. The lap distances in the FIA PU information document match the data frame at zero offset (exit T1 400–550 m, exit T9 3000–3300 m, T11–T14 3700–4150 m, overtake detection 5560 m = exit T17). Sector lines use the FIA map lengths 1.823 / 2.464 / 1.604 km, scaled by 5880.66/5891. The speed trap is 140 m before T15 (Stowe). Intermediate 1 is 110 m before T6, intermediate 2 is 50 m after T14 (taken at the S2 line), and the finish line is at the control line. Uncertainty is about ±50 m: the sim's finish-line speed is low (§6), which would fit a line ~50 m later.
- **Straight Mode zones:** the FIA activation points A1 (65 m after T18), A2 (55 m after T5), A3 (155 m after T7) and A4 (65 m after T14) are placed from the racing-line apexes, giving s 22, 1376, 2397 and 4323. The deactivation points are not published, so each zone runs to the next named corner (T1, T6, T9, T15).

## 3. Car model and equations

Point mass m = mass_kg + fuel_kg, effective mass m_e = 1.03 m (rotating parts). Air speed u = v + w, where w is the headwind component along the racing-line heading (same convention as `lab/aero.js headwind`). q = ½ρu².

1. **Aero load per axle:** F_zf = m g w_f + q·CLA_f, F_zr = m g (1 − w_f) + q·CLA_r. In Straight Mode the straight coefficients are used, capped at the Corner Mode values (C3.10.10 n / C3.11.6 c: the flaps only lower incidence, so Straight Mode can never add load or drag). A stalled floor gives CLA × 0.5.
2. **Tyres** (load-sensitive, per axle): the per-tyre friction is μ(F_z) = μ₀·k_grip·(1 + ls − ls·F_z/F_z0), with F_z0 = static load per tyre and ls = `load_sens` (fractional loss of μ per unit of load ratio). Lateral load transfer ΔF = m_axle·a_y·h/track (split by axle mass share) gives an axle capacity of μ₀[F_z(1+ls) − ls(F_z²/2 + 2ΔF²)/F_z0], which is solved in closed form for a_y,max per axle. The car's a_y,max is the smaller of the two axles (the aero balance matters).
3. **Friction ellipse:** a_x,avail = a_x,max·√(1 − (a_y/a_y,max)²). Traction comes from the rear axle (μ_x). Braking uses both axles with ideal balance, capped at `max_decel_g_mech`·m g.
4. **Resistances:** drag ½ρu²·CDA·k_drag, rolling C_rr·(m g + aero load) with C_rr = 0.012, grade m g sinθ.
5. **Power:** P_wheel = η_dt·(P_ICE − P_clip/0.97) + η_dt·0.97·P_K. P_K is at most min(mguk_kw, C5.2.8 taper: 1800 − 5v below 340 km/h, 6900 − 20v to 345 km/h, then 0; Overtake curve 7100 − 20v). F = min(P_wheel/v, traction limit). The K deploys only the part that traction can use.
6. **Apex limits:** the largest v with v²|κ| ≤ a_y,max(v + w), in Corner Mode, found by bisection.
7. **Backward pass (braking):** v_i² = v_{i+1}² + 2Δs·(F_brake + D + R + m g sinθ)/m_e, in Corner Mode (the wings close on the brakes). It starts from the slowest apex and runs one lap.
8. **Forward pass (traction):** v_{i+1}² = v_i² + 2Δs·(F − D − R − m g sinθ)/m_e, bounded by the backward pass. The car uses Straight Mode inside an FIA zone once the ≤ 400 ms switch has completed (it is in Corner Mode during the switch), and drops back to Corner Mode if lateral demand exceeds 50 % of Straight Mode grip. Where braking binds, the K harvests min(350 kW, 0.97·η_dt·0.43·F_brake·v) (43 % rear brake share).
9. **Energy (per lap):** deployed ≤ 4 MJ (C5.2.9 state-of-charge window) + harvested, and harvested ≤ min(car `es_harvest_mj_per_lap`, conditions `recharge_limit_mj`, 8.5 MJ C5.2.10). Silverstone qualifying is 6.5 MJ. The state-of-charge trajectory within the lap is not tracked (lap totals only).
10. **Strategy:**
    - `"threshold"` (default, calibrated): the K deploys while v < v\* and superclips (harvests at full throttle) while v ≥ v\*. The superclip power share is uniform, set so superclip fills what braking leaves of the recharge cap. v\* is solved by bisection so the energy budget closes, and the braking/superclip split is iterated (3 rounds). This gives the speed plateau that real 2026 qualifying traps show: 292–318 km/h in qualifying against 305–352 km/h in the race.
    - `"optimal"`: an equal-marginal-value rule (deploy where Σds/v³ to the next braking point is high, superclip where it is lowest). In this model it is ~1.1 s faster but gives a ~341 km/h trap, which the real qualifying data does not show. It is offered for exploration and is not calibrated.
11. **Lap:** the lap time is Σ 2Δs/(v_i + v_{i+1}). Sectors and the trap are taken in the FIA frame (§2).

Gears and rpm are not modelled: power is applied as a continuous P/v. MGU-K torque (500 Nm, C5.2.11) is not modelled either; it would only bind at low speed, where traction already limits.

## 5. Calibration

**Global (2 factors, 2 targets):** k_grip scales μ_x and μ_y; k_drag scales both CDAs. The targets are ANT's pole lap of 88.111 s and his qualifying speed trap of 302.6 km/h (`EI.RACEPACE.silverstone`, `speedTrapQuali`). The solver is a damped 2×2 Newton.

| Step | k_grip | k_drag | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8.5 MJ, 20 °C ISA-like air, game sector thirds, curvature-detected zones | 1.337 | 1.024 | first version |
| + qualifying weather (ρ 1.170), **6.5 MJ** Q recharge, FIA timing lines | 1.422 | 1.252 | less superclip energy, so the plateau rises and more drag is needed to hit 302.6 |
| + FIA zones A1–A4 (A3 adds Woodcote–Copse in Straight Mode) | 1.393 | 1.310 | |
| + 400 ms Corner → Straight switch | **1.397** | **1.303** | committed (`fitted.js`) |

Result: baseline 88.113 s (+0.002 s), trap 302.6 km/h. k_grip ≈ 1.4 is large. It absorbs the tight OSM racing-line radii (§2), the tyre μ assumption and anything else that makes real cars faster in corners than this point mass. k_drag ≈ 1.3 says that either the assumed CDAs are ~30 % low or the real qualifying energy strategy derates harder than the threshold model. The data cannot separate the two.

## 6. Competitor model (inferred team cars)

**These per-team car parameters are inferred from public lap times and speed traps, not from team data.** For each team, the driver with the team's best qualifying lap is taken (any session Q1–Q3), together with that driver's qualifying speed trap (`EI.RACEPACE.silverstone`). Teams and power units come from `EI.SEASON.teams`. The free parameters are, per team, a CLA scale (both modes, both axles; balance kept) and a CDA scale, plus one ICE-power scale per PU maker (shared by customer teams): 27 parameters for 22 data points. The fit is a maximum a posteriori estimate (Levenberg–Marquardt) with likelihood widths of 0.10 s on the lap and 1.0 km/h on the trap, log-normal priors centred on the baseline (σ 0.12 / 0.10 / 0.03), and hard bounds CLA 0.70–1.15, CDA 0.80–1.25, PU 0.92–1.05. Driver skill, session and track evolution, setup and energy strategy are all absorbed into these car numbers.

## 7. Limitations (biggest first)

1. **Geometry:** the OSM centreline with a 15 m corridor, smoothed. Fast-corner radii are probably underestimated (Abbey 222 km/h against near-flat in reality), and one global grip factor compensates on average. S2 is +0.34 s and S1/S3 are −0.1/−0.2 s against the real pole lap.
2. **Energy strategy:** the "threshold" deploy/superclip rule is a calibrated behavioural model, not an optimal control solution. The state-of-charge trajectory within a lap is not tracked. Above the 6.5 MJ qualifying limit (for example race-like 8.5 MJ) forced superclipping makes the lap slower in this model (+0.15 s at 8.5 MJ), so do not use the threshold mode to study race energy. A test checks that the lap is monotone in recharge up to 6.5 MJ.
3. **Point mass:** there is no yaw or transient dynamics. Longitudinal load transfer is ignored, brake balance is ideal, and lateral load transfer is split by axle mass share. There are no gears, no rpm and no MGU-K torque limit. There is no tyre temperature or wear, and no track-temperature effect.
4. **Calibration:** 2 global factors from 2 numbers (one lap, one trap). k_grip ≈ 1.4 and k_drag ≈ 1.3 are model-error absorbers, not physical tyre or aero claims.
5. **Team cars:** inferred from one lap and one trap per team. Driver, session (Q1 against Q3) and strategy are mixed into the car numbers. Treat them as a field of plausible competitors, not as copies of team cars.
6. **Wind:** headwind component only (no crosswind effects). The qualifying wind direction is unpublished, so the default is 0. Wet is a single μ factor.
7. **Ride height:** CLA is taken at the design ride height. There is no heave, rake or speed-dependent ride height. A stalled floor is a flat 50 % CLA loss.

## 8. Timing and determinism

- One lap: ~13–15 ms in Node 22 after warm-up (about 60 forward passes for the energy closure). The test requires < 30 ms.
- The first call builds the track (racing line) once in ~0.9 s, after which it is cached.
- `node concept/sim/build-fit.js` refits everything in ~9 s and writes `fitted.js` byte-identically for the same code and data. `--check` exits non-zero if the committed file is stale.
- Every output carries the label above. The sim makes no network calls and no Mubit calls, and never reads `data/` or `data-mock/`.
