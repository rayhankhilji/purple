# CONCEPT lap simulator (Silverstone), v3

Every output carries this label: **"physics lap sim · quasi-steady-state point mass · Silverstone OSM centreline · calibrated to 2026 British GP qualifying"** (the label string is kept for its consumers; the sim resolves loads per axle, see §3).

This is the deterministic benchmark the car-design optimiser calls. It computes one flying qualifying lap (time, sectors, speed / gear / rpm / time trace, per-corner speeds, energy use) from a car's physical numbers: mass, aero coefficients per active-aero mode and the aero platform, PU power and the 2026 energy rules, tyres, brakes and gearing. **Speeds come out of the physics and are never fitted as targets, and there are no per-corner factors anywhere.** Telemetry is used for two things only:

1. Estimating parameters nobody publishes, on **one** lap (the pole lap's speed trace and lap time), inside documented prior ranges (§4.2: the sourced ranges of `concept/data/priors-2026.json`, three of them widened with the evidence stated).
2. A **held-out check** on 10 other teams' laps, scored with the track model's `compare()` (`concept/track/track.js`).

Per-team car parameters are inferred from public lap times and speed traps. They are not team data.

Code is in `concept/sim/` (plain JS for Node and the browser). Tests: `test/concept-sim.test.js`, `test/concept-sim-v2.test.js`, `test/concept-sim-v3.test.js`. Overlay figures: `docs/concept/sim-trace-{ant,had,sai}.svg`.

**The accuracy targets are not met.** RMS fell by 2.3 km/h and the S2 bias vanished, but S1 (+0.17 s) and S3 (−0.19 s) remain just outside 0.15 s and the speed RMS is 10.1 km/h, not 7. §7 says what is left and why it is not a parameter problem.

What changed, each from the regulations or the physics, never a per-corner factor:

1. **Energy (biggest):** a new default strategy `"regulated"`: deployment planned by the lap-time value of a joule (the costate of v2's `"optimal"`), then shaped by the **C5.12.4–C5.12.7 power-reduction rules** and the Silverstone sector tables of the FIA PU information document (§3.4). Real 2026 traces plateau and fade on every straight; v3 reproduces that shape where v2's speed threshold produced a flat ceiling.
2. **Tyres:** power-law load sensitivity (μ ∝ (F_z/F_z0)^−ls, F_y ∝ F_z^(1−ls), the form of Milliken's load-sensitivity data) with the lateral load transfer solved per axle, replacing the linear law (§3.2); the slip angle at the limit is now estimated inside a sourced range.
3. **Priors re-examined (§4.2):** the corner-mode CLA cap (4.6 m²) and the CdA caps cannot be reconciled with the telemetry; they are widened, with the evidence, and every fitted value is reported against both the sourced and the widened range. The braking data term now comes from the telemetry itself (the brake supplier's simulated 3.1–3.8 g is contradicted by the measured ~4 g).
4. **Racing line:** laps averaged by position (projection onto the current mean line, two passes) instead of by lap fraction (§2).
5. **FIA:** legal track widths (C10.10.1), and the PhysicalCar's tracks, wheel positions and floor throat legal (docs/concept/FIA_CHECKLIST.md).
6. **Held-out score of record:** `fitted.js` now stores each lap's `track.compare()` result (`heldout.compare`), and a test bounds it.

- **`conditions`:** `{ air_temp_c, track_temp_c, air_density_kgm3?, air_pressure_hpa?, humidity_pct?, wind_ms, wind_from_deg, wet, fuel_kg?, recharge_limit_mj?, es_start_mj? }`. - **`opts`:** `trace`, `debug`, `strategy` (`"regulated"` default; `"optimal"`, `"threshold"` (the v2 default), `"marginal"`), `overtake`, `no_superclip`, `part_throttle_harvest` (default true), `line` (`"telemetry"` default, `"osm"`), `grade_scale` (default 1), `cal` (identity).
- **PhysicalCar fields the sim reads** (all read defensively; **new in v3 marked ‡**): `mass_kg, fuel_kg, wheelbase_m, track_front_m, track_rear_m, cog_height_m, weight_dist_front, drivetrain_eff`; `aero.corner|straight.{CLA_front_m2, CLA_rear_m2, CDA_m2}`, `aero.floor_stalled` (or top-level), `aero.ride_sens_per_mm`, `aero.stall_margin_mm`, `aero.heave_n_per_mm`, `aero.floor_share`, `aero.yaw_sens`; `pu.{ice_kw, mguk_kw, es_deploy_mj_per_lap, es_harvest_mj_per_lap, superclip_kw?}`; `tyres.{mu_x, mu_y, load_sens, slip_angle_rad‡}` (`slip_angle_rad` optional: absent = `MODEL.slip_angle_rad` 0.08; `concept/physical.js` now copies it from the anchor car); `brakes.max_decel_g_mech`, `brakes.bias_front?`, `brakes.bias_ref?`; `gearing.{rpm_per_kmh[8], shift_rpm, rev_limit_rpm?}`; `suspension.lltd_front?` (more-factors v1, §3.4b). The meaning of `tyres.load_sens` is unchanged (fraction of μ lost per unit of load ratio at the static load); only the law's shape changed (§3.2).
- **Determinism:** fixed iteration counts, Float64, no randomness, no network, no Mubit, never touches `data/` or `data-mock/`.

## 2. Track (corner by corner)

- **Racing line:** the average path of the 22 qualifying laps (OpenF1 X/Y, `concept/data/telemetry`), mapped into the OSM frame of `js/data/silverstone.js` (Procrustes: reflection, 1.38° rotation, 5.3 m RMS). **v3:** the laps are first averaged at equal lap fractions, then twice re-averaged **by position**: every lap's points are projected onto the current mean line (nearest point, searched around the fraction's index) and the median lateral offset per 5 m station moves the line. Averaging by fraction alone mixes points up to ~15 m apart along the track. The radii barely move (Stowe 86 → 87 m, Brooklands 57 → 56 m, Maggotts 143 → 136 m); the estimator is the more defensible one. Smoothing σ = one position-sample interval of travel (max(6 m, v·0.26 s)); curvature σ = 4 m; 2.5 m grid (2330 points).
- **Line radii checked against an independent estimate:** the track model's racing line (OSM centreline + the field's mean lateral offset, TRACK.md) gives the same or tighter radii at the corners where the sim is slow (Stowe 81–85 m, Brooklands 53–54 m, Becketts T13 78–81 m) and much tighter fast corners (the OSM centreline's kinks). The sim's line is not too tight. Less smoothing (σ × 0.35–0.6) makes the mid-speed corners slower still and the fit worse (tried).
- **Track width and kerbs, elevation, banking, timing frame, Straight Mode zones:** unchanged from v2 (the telemetry line includes the kerbs; gradient from an undocumented profile, an estimate; no banking; control line at s = 0 on the pole lap's 5824.6 m, sector lines 1807.5 / 4239.1 m, trap 4780 m; FIA activation points A1–A4).

## 3. Model

Air speed u = v + headwind (per segment heading; same convention as `lab/aero.js`), q = ½ρu². Every aero load and drag force is evaluated at u.

### 3.1 Aero platform

Unchanged from v2: squat dh = F / k_heave (3500 N/mm, assumption), CLA × (1 + ride_sens·dh), floor share rolls off past the stall onset (5 mm + `stall_margin_mm`). Tested in v3 as a source of the high-speed grip saturation the telemetry suggests (§7): a softer heave (1100–2000 N/mm) with a narrower stall window did not change the held-out RMS (± 0.4 km/h), so it is not used.

### 3.2 Tyres and axle grip with load transfer (v3)

- **Power-law load sensitivity (new):** per tyre, peak force F = μ F_z0 (F_z / F_z0)^(1 − ls), i.e. μ(F_z) = μ (F_z/F_z0)^−ls. This is the form of the load-sensitivity data in Milliken & Milliken (*Race Car Vehicle Dynamics*, as quoted by Wikipedia, "Tire load sensitivity": F_y ∝ F_z^n, n ≈ 0.7–0.9; their example falls from μ 1.10 to 0.97 when the load doubles, n ≈ 0.82). Its slope at the static load equals the v2 linear law's, so `load_sens` (and the prior in %/kN) keeps its meaning; at high aero load it keeps more grip than the linear law, which turns down like a parabola and, with load transfer, cost too much (200 km/h: 3.85 g against 3.59 g). `MODEL.tyre_law = "linear"` restores v2.
- **Lateral:** per axle, the two tyres at F_z/2 ± ΔF, ΔF = m_axle·a_y·h/track; the axle limit solves m_axle·a = F(a) by 6 Newton steps (deterministic); a_y,max is the smaller axle limit.
- **Longitudinal:** braking and traction capacity per axle with the same law; longitudinal load transfer m·a_x·h/wheelbase as v2; braking capped by `max_decel_g_mech`.
- **Friction ellipse** couples a_x and a_y. Traction is rear-only.
- **Tyre slip drag:** m·a_y·tan(α), α = α_lim × (share of the lateral limit used). v3 estimates α_lim with the baseline (§5) inside a **sourced range 0.05–0.16 rad**: F1 tyres reach peak side force at about 3° (Wikipedia, "Tire load sensitivity") and the academic F1 tyre model peaks at 8–9° (Perantoni & Limebeer, Table 3; `priors-2026.json` `mu_peak_load_table_academic`). Estimate: 0.098 rad (5.6°).
- Track temperature, crosswind yaw, wet: unchanged (estimates, §4.3).

### 3.3 Resistance and power

Unchanged: drag ½ρu²·CDA, rolling 0.012·(m g + aero load), grade, slip drag; P_wheel = η_dt·(P_ICE(N) − P_clip/0.97) + η_dt·0.97·P_K; P_K ≤ min(350 kW, C5.2.8 taper); P_ICE below 10,500 rpm limited by (0.27N + 165)/3000 (C5.2.4); 30 ms upshift cut (assumption).

### 3.4 Energy: the `"regulated"` strategy (v3 default)

The 2026 rules modelled: C5.2.7 (350 kW), C5.2.8 taper, C5.2.9 4 MJ window, C5.2.10 recharge cap (6.5 MJ in Silverstone qualifying), C5.2.21 (0.97), and **new: C5.12.4–C5.12.7** (FIA 2026 Section C Issue 19, p. C74–C75; identical in Issue 21) with the event's tables (FIA 2026 British GP Power Unit Information, `concept/data/silverstone-2026-weekend.json` `energy_limits`):

| Rule | Text (Issue 19) | In the sim |
|---|---|---|
| C5.12.4 | the driver maximum power demand cannot be reduced by more than 150 kW at the start of a power-limited pending period; the reduction is held ≥ 1 s; exceptions for specified sectors | `REG_RATE.step_kw` 150, `hold_s` 1; 350 kW in the four listed sectors (T1–T4 350–1000 m, T6–T7 1950–2100, T15–T16 4950–5200, Exit T17 5550–5750 in Q) |
| C5.12.5 | no increase during a power-limited period except Boost or a permitted reset | the command is never raised inside a period, except to follow the plan when it asks for more power (Boost, a driver-selected mode) and in the reset sectors (Exit T1 400–550, Exit T9 3000–3300, Exit T17–T18 5650–5900 in Q); a period ends with a real lift (braking, or under half the ICE power) |
| C5.12.6(a) | ≤ 50 kW per 1 s while ERS-K DC power > 100 kW, where the power-limited distance exceeds 3500 m (Silverstone: 3833 m); total reduction ≤ 700 kW | `rate_kw_s` 50, `free_below_kw` 100; superclip ≤ 350 kW keeps the total under 700 kW |
| C5.12.7 | ERS-K may drop faster when the rate-limited value would be < 100 kW, when the driver demand is negative, for the C5.2.8 taper, during a shift, or below 210 km/h (300 km/h in T11–T14 3700–4150 m, 230 km/h in T18 5600–5800 m) | the command drops at once to the plan below the sector's speed threshold or once it is ≤ 100 kW; the C5.2.8 taper always applies on top |

Sector distances are on the FIA's 5891 m lap, mapped onto the sim's s by length ratio (≤ 20 m error).

**The plan.** As in v2's `"optimal"`: on a reference lap (deploy everywhere) the costate of kinetic energy, sens(s) = Σ ds/v³ carried back along each full-throttle run with the drag and power derivatives, is the lap time a joule buys at s. The plan deploys a share fd = logistic(log sens − log x) of the available ERS-K power and superclips a share fc = logistic(log y − log sens) of the clip power (≤ 350 kW, Miami-onwards figure, PRIORS.md), y = min(x·0.97², the level whose cheapest joules fill the recharge cap). Two v3 additions: (1) at each point the plan values a joule by sens × (v_ref/v)³, the car's own speed against the reference lap's, so a car slowed by clipping or drag values energy more and never crawls; (2) the logistic widths are a **policy** setting, `MODEL.reg_soft_deploy` / `reg_soft_clip` = 0.5 in log sens (0.35 and 0.75 tried: 10.13 / 10.70 km/h on the fit lap against 10.26): a team does not run a bang-bang deployment, and the measured traces imply ERS-K levels of ~150–250 kW during accelerations and 50–250 kW of superclip on the plateaus (power balance on the pole lap with the fitted drag), which the soft plan reproduces (deployment peaks ~250 kW in the sim). A superclip may not decelerate the car by more than `MODEL.clip_decel_max` = 3 m/s² at full throttle (model bound; the measured full-throttle fades are ≤ ~2.5 m/s², TRACK.md clipping table).

**The rules then shape it.** When the plan first asks for less than the available power, a power-limited period starts: the command steps down by at most 150 kW (350 kW in the listed sectors), sized to what the plan wants 1 s later, holds 1 s, then falls at ≤ 50 kW/s while above 100 kW and above the speed threshold. The store is tracked from the control line (start 4 MJ, never empty, harvest stored only below the window top and the recharge cap); x is set by bisection on that ledger. `test/concept-sim-v3.test.js` checks the rate rule on the simulated lap point by point and that lifting the rate limits only makes the lap faster.

**What it changes.** The straights now peak and fade like the real traces (pit straight, Wellington, National, Hangar), the Stowe→Vale run no longer overshoots by 36 km/h (now +1.5 / −3.7 km/h peak / end), and the sector bias in S2 (+0.50 s in v2) is gone. The baseline lap deploys 8.15 MJ, harvests 4.15 MJ (1.86 braking, 2.20 superclip, 0.09 part throttle) and ends with an empty store.

The other strategies are kept: `"threshold"` (v2 default, speed ceiling), `"optimal"` (v2: bang-bang by sens, no C5.12), `"marginal"`.

### 3.4b Set-up and gearing (more-factors v1, docs/concept/FACTORS.md)

Each term is read only when the car carries its field. The RB22 team car now carries the RB22 data sheet's inputs (concept/sim/fit.js
`derivedOverrides`: mass 775 kg, the eight ratios measured on all 25 Red Bull laps, the sheet's 55 % roll split as `suspension.lltd_front`
0.5215), and `fitTeams` fits every team with its own such inputs merged in. Refit (`build-fit.js`, after the plan smoothing below):
generic baseline **88.100 s** (pole 88.111), `baselineCar("redbull")` **88.745 s** (HAD 88.746), held-out speed RMS 9.88 km/h, sector
bias +0.144 / +0.042 / −0.191 s; baseline estimate μy 1.9455, μx 1.7249, CLA 5.043, CdA 1.175 / 0.953 (the sheet check now reports one
contradiction: μy 1.9455 against the sheet's 1.80 ± 0.10).

- **Lateral load transfer distribution** (`suspension.lltd_front`): the front axle carries `lltd` of the transfer moment `m a h`
  (`dF = lltd·m·a·h / track_front`), the rear `1 − lltd`; each axle must still produce `m_axle·a`. Absent: `lltd = weight split`
  (the v3 behaviour). With it, the rear axle's lateral limit at the reference split over its limit at this split (`RK`) scales the
  friction-ellipse utilisation the traction limit uses: transfer moved onto the rear costs exit traction.
- **Brake balance** (`brakes.bias_front`, `brakes.bias_ref`, default reference 1 − `rear_brake_share` = 0.57): capacity at a fixed
  balance is `min(F_front/b, F_rear/(1 − b))` with the longitudinal transfer at the car's own deceleration; the calibrated capacity
  is scaled by that over the same at the reference. The MGU-K's braking harvest uses the rear share `1 − b`.
- **Platform stiffness** (`aero.heave_n_per_mm`, existing): the stall onset (as a squat) is now `5 mm × 3500 / k + margin`, the
  geometric gap the design layer's margin was judged at; before, it was `5 + margin` whatever `k` (no car set `k`).
- **Gearing** (`MODEL.rev`, estimates): above the car's shift rpm ICE power falls 3 % per 1000 rpm; drive (ICE and MGU-K) is cut
  over the last 200 rpm before a 13 000 rpm limiter (`gearing.rev_limit_rpm` overrides). Only a top gear too short for the straight
  reaches either.
- **Plan smoothing** (more-factors v1; FACTORS.md §4 has the trace that found each): the `"regulated"` plan had five hard switches
  that made the lap jump between nearby cars (the RB22 sat on a local peak in mass; +0.5 kg was 0.058 s faster). Now: part-throttle
  harvest and the costate reset at grip-limited points scale with the speed the limit takes off the step (`sens_reset_dv` 1.5 m/s);
  the ERS-K reaching 100 kW inside a rate-limited period is time-weighted within the step; the clip threshold is interpolated within
  the point that fills the recharge allowance; feasibility uses a soft minimum over the store's troughs (`soc_soft_mj` 0.03 MJ,
  conservative); the C5.12.7 speed threshold is blended over ±3 km/h (`reg_vthr_band_kmh`); a period's initial step scales with the
  dip that starts it (`reg_start_ramp_kw` 20 kW). Mass is now monotone over ±4 kg in 0.5 kg steps (0.0195 s/kg on the RB22).

### 3.5 Passes

Apex limits from a_y,max by bisection; backward braking pass in Corner Mode; forward pass from the slowest apex with the strategy. Straight Mode only inside an FIA zone, after a ≤ 400 ms switch, closed when lateral demand exceeds 50 % of Straight Mode grip and always under braking.

**Smoothness:** a 0.25 % CLA step moves the lap by about −0.02 s with up to ±0.015 s of noise around that trend (the store's binding point can move between braking zones); the test bound is 0.05 s per step (v2: 0.035). Pure physics with the energy not limiting is smooth to ~0.014 s per 0.1 %.

**Why CLA is widened (and the CdA caps with it).** The sourced cap (4.6 m²) is "academic 4.5 and blog fits 5.8–6.6 for 2022–25 cars, × 0.70 (FIA −30 %)" (PRIORS.md). The blog fits were made with that author's own tyre friction (CLA and μ trade 1 : 1 in such fits, IDENTIFY.md) on 2022 cars, before three seasons of development; the FIA's −30 % is against the cars current when it was written. Against that, the telemetry on the measured line (two independent line estimates, §2) demands at the corners' tightest points: Brooklands ~3.8–4.1 g at ~165–176 km/h, Becketts T13 ~4.2–4.9 g at ~210–229 km/h, Stowe ~4.5–5.2 g at ~221–240 km/h (field-mean to ANT speeds), while Abbey and Copse are flat at 296–307 km/h. With μ ≤ 2.0, load sensitivity ≥ 3 %/kN and CLA ≤ 4.6 the v2 model reaches 3.0 g at 160 and 4.2 g at 240 km/h (the "20–25 km/h slow" of v2). The independent 9-circuit identification (IDENTIFY.md) finds ClA 6.3–8.1 m² effective on the same kind of curvature. The fit, given room up to 6.5, takes 5.32 m². The **CdA** caps (1.25 / 1.0) are widened because, with drag at those caps, the speed traces imply the cars deploy ~4 MJ and superclip ~3.5 MJ at full throttle (power balance with ICE 400 kW), i.e. finish a pole lap with charge left, and the 300–316 km/h plateaus imply a straight-mode CdA near 1.0 m² on ICE alone; IDENTIFY finds 1.15–1.46 m². The fit takes 1.18 / 0.95, inside the sourced caps after all, so only CLA ends outside its sourced range.

**Braking data term (replaced).** v2 pulled the car towards the brake supplier's simulated 3.4 g at 260 km/h (Brembo, 3.1–3.8). The raw OpenF1 car_data samples contradict it: the Village stop (from ~270 km/h) shows a per-lap peak deceleration over one sample interval (~0.27 s) of median **4.01 g at ~226 km/h** across the 22 laps (2.9–6.2), Vale 3.8 g at ~190 km/h. v3 uses the measured value (sd 0.5 g) as the data term (`BRAKE_TELEMETRY`, recomputed by `brakeTelemetry()`, checked by a test). ## 5. Estimation on one lap (no correction factors)

MAP, Nelder–Mead (420 iterations, two deterministic starting points: the prior centre and a high-downforce / high-drag corner of the box, then 2 restarts from the best) over 9 parameters (μ_y, μ_x, CLA, balance, CDA, CDA_straight, CLA straight ratio, load sensitivity, slip angle). Each is mapped to its fit range by a logistic transform with a Gaussian prior centred on the sourced central value, sd = range/4. Objective: speed RMS² on the pole lap's 5 m grid + 400·(lap error)² + 10·(prior + data terms); data terms = the derived lateral limits and CdA (§4.2) and the telemetry braking peak.

**Fit lap (ANT, 88.111 s):** sim 88.107 s, sectors 28.367 / 35.674 / 24.066 against 28.348 / 35.521 / 24.242, trap 310.2 against 302 km/h, speed RMS 10.26 km/h (v2: 13.1).

## 7. Limitations (biggest first)

1. **Copse → Becketts runs fast (+10–19 km/h over ~800 m), and the mid-speed corners Brooklands (−19.5) and The Loop / Luffield (−10) stay slow.** Together these are about half of the remaining squared error. The real cars arrive at Copse ~15 km/h slower than the sim after superclipping hard from ~2850 m, and they do not deploy after Copse; the sim, with Copse not grip-limiting (its capability at 300 km/h is > 6 g), sees the National straight, Copse and Maggotts as one long run and deploys through it. Either the real cars' grip saturates above ~240 km/h (Copse, Abbey and Stowe all need ~5–5.5 g on the measured line, nearly independent of speed; a downforce or tyre saturation the sourced physics does not give: §3.1 tried the floor-stall route) or the teams' energy policy is shaped by something not public (FIA-F1-DOC-058 defines the power-limited periods). It is not a parameter problem: no single car inside or outside the widened ranges fixes both ends (manual scans, CLA 3.3–6.5, μ 1.85–2.5, load sensitivity 0–6 %/kN).
2. **Slow complexes:** Village and Vale run fast by ~7 km/h while The Loop and Luffield run slow by ~10–12: a single grip curve cannot serve all four at the line's radii (Village 42 m at 108 km/h needs 2.2 g, Luffield 35 m at 114 km/h needs 2.9 g). Transients (yaw, rotation, kerbs) that a quasi-steady model does not have.
3. **Sector bias S1 +0.17 s, S3 −0.19 s** follows from 1 and 2 (S1 holds The Loop and Brooklands, S3 the Vale–Club complex and the lap end).
4. **Priors:** CLA is outside its sourced range (5.32 against 4.6) on the evidence in §4.2. Treat the CLA and CdA levels as effective values of this model (the same caveat IDENTIFY.md gives); the relative effects of a design change are what the optimiser uses.
5. **Geometry estimates:** gradient from an undocumented elevation profile; no banking; one averaged line for every car.
6. **Quasi-steady state:** no yaw or transient dynamics, no tyre temperature build-up or wear, ideal brake balance unless the car declares one (§3.4b; then one fixed balance for the lap, no driver trim or migration); yaw inertia, unsprung masses and material flex (in the PhysicalCar) are not used. Aero balance does not shift with speed or ride height.
7. **Team cars:** driver, session, tow and strategy are folded into three scales per team.

## 8. Timing and regeneration

- more-factors v1 adds ~1 ms (the `RK` and fixed-balance tables): ~20-23 ms a lap measured while the test suite ran.
- One lap: ~21 ms CPU (regulated), ~19 ms (optimal), ~30 ms (threshold) in Node 22 after warm-up; test < 30 ms. The power-law tyre tables take ~3 ms of that. The first call builds the track in ~1 s.
- `node concept/sim/build-fit.js` re-estimates, refits and re-checks in ~80–110 s, byte-deterministic; `--check` exits non-zero if `fitted.js` is stale; it prints the held-out `track.compare()` summary. `node concept/sim/plot-trace.js ANT HAD SAI` redraws the overlays. `node concept/sim/build-line.js` rebuilds the line (`--fraction-only` for the v2 averaging).
- Telemetry comes from OpenF1 (openf1.org), an independent community project not associated with Formula 1, the FIA or FOM, used for non-commercial research.
