# Public priors for the 2026 car-identification fit

Researched 2026-10-24 (every source accessed that day). Data with full source records: `concept/data/priors-2026.json`. Regulation items are cited by their key in `concept/data/regs-2026-key.json`, not repeated; what is already known about the RB22 is in `docs/concept/REDBULL.md`.

**Kinds:** official (FIA, Pirelli, Brembo, F1, team statement, regulation text), reported (journalism, blogs, forums, Wikipedia, third-party simulations), academic (papers), engineering-estimate (arithmetic on cited numbers or my judgement; the method is in the JSON `how`). Sources marked `[S]` were taken from a search summary only and are unconfirmed; all others were read in the page. `not public` means searched and nothing found. The last column and the final table are my judgement, labelled as such, not public numbers.

## Headline findings

- **Nominal Tyre Mass: not found.** No final value was published in any source reached. Only estimates: 46 kg (FIA 2024, 722 + 46 = 768), 44 kg (RaceFans assumption), 46.4 kg (The Race, 726 + 46.4 = 772.4). Declared car masses bound it at about 48 kg or less. Recommended prior 44-48 kg, central 46.
- **2026 tyre sizes:** front 280 mm wide, 705 mm diameter; rear 375 mm, 710 mm (Mercedes F1 page; Pirelli gives the same deltas). The four tyres together are 1.6 kg lighter than 2025 (front -300 g each, rear -500 g each).
- **FIA aero targets:** downforce -30 %, drag -55 % versus 'current cars' at the time (the 2022-2025 generation). Not stated: trim, circuit, or whether Corner or Straight Mode. Absolute CdA and ClA are not public for any car.
- **Straight Mode, third-party simulations:** drag -18 % and -21 %, downforce -25 % and -31 % (two unrelated simulations; neither is a team figure).
- **Braking at Silverstone (Brembo simulation):** peak 2.9 g (T3) and 3.1 g (T16) in 2026, versus 4.5 g at T3 in 2025. Three brake applications, just over 6.5 s per lap.
- **ICE power:** about 400 kW by FIA design statement; press estimates 387-430 kW by manufacturer, with a method that assumes equal drag.

## Conflicts and cautions

- **ICE ranking:** ScuderiaFans (20 Apr 2026) puts Mercedes highest at about 430 kW; the FIA's ADUO ranking (docs/concept/REDBULL.md) puts Mercedes 2-4 % below the best ICE, reported to be Red Bull Ford. Do not use the per-team order.
- **Recharge limits:** Miami figures (8.5 / 9 / 8 MJ, PlanetF1) differ from the FIA Silverstone document in the repo (8.0 / 8.5 race, 6.5 Q). Limits are set per event; use the Silverstone document.
- **Straight/Corner CdA:** the repo model uses 1.10 / 0.97 (ratio 0.88). The two public simulations give a ratio of 0.79-0.82, which would put straight-mode CdA near 0.87-0.90 with the same corner value of 1.10. The FIA's -55 % applied to a 2022-style CdA of 1.35 gives 0.61.
- **Sources used only through search summaries** (marked `[S]`): Red Bull's guide (0.3-0.4 s per 10 kg, NTM definition), FIA Issue 8 text, Monza and Baku braking, Brembo 2020 disc peaks, Isola temperature windows, 2025 Silverstone pole time, the compression-ratio claim, forum posts. Confirm before quoting.
- The Michelin 'point' of downforce or drag is undefined in the article; do not convert it to a coefficient.
- All academic aero and tyre numbers describe pre-2022 or model cars; the blog fit couples ClA with tyre grip.
